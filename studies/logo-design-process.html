Identity notes · 01The Word / Logo process

A small mark with a generous spirit

Designing a logo that feels like welcome.

The process begins with the identity already in place: four softly rounded panes, a slight turn, a brass-edged serif wordmark, and an open page of color. The work is to make that mark feel unmistakably The Word: warm without being sentimental, regal without being distant, and playful without losing reverence.

01—06

The design process

A disciplined sequence keeps the identity meaningful at every size and honest in every context. Each decision has a reason; decoration earns its place.

01

Listen before drawing

Gather the full character brief and the existing brand. Find its productive tensions: authority with tenderness, beauty with usefulness, sacred purpose with everyday ease. Do not reduce the identity to a single mood word.

02

Choose a durable idea

Use the four-pane mark as a compact image of a page, a window, and a place gathered around shared words. Let the open, balanced geometry communicate welcome; keep its meaning suggestive rather than over-explained.

03

Refine the silhouette

Set a consistent 2 × 2 grid, tune corner radii optically, and give every pane enough room to remain distinct at favicon size. Keep the slight rotation energetic, but ensure the mark still sits steadily beside the wordmark.

04

Make color carry meaning

Keep blue as the anchor for trust and clear thought. Let celadon, rose, and lavender add tenderness and wit. Use brass as a measured note of warmth and regal craft, not as a substitute for contrast.

05

Pair sign with word

Give the serif wordmark enough presence to feel literary and elegant. Balance it with a restrained interface face for edition labels. Test the lockup as a unit, then test the symbol alone where space is scarce.

06

Test, simplify, and sign off

Review in color, one color, tiny sizes, on light and dark surfaces, and alongside real scripture text. Ask readers whether it feels kind, reliable, beautiful, and clear. Remove any flourish that makes the mark harder to recognize or use.

PROOF

How the mark earns trust

A beautiful logo should be dependable in ordinary use. These checks turn the intention into practical standards.

Recognition

At 16–32 pixels, the four panes still read as one mark. At larger sizes, their subtle differences reward a closer look.

Accessibility

Small labels remain legible; meaning never depends on color alone. The silhouette stays clear in grayscale and for low-vision readers.

Reverence

Faith is treated with humility. The identity suggests reflection and welcome without using sacred symbols as ornament or making promises the product cannot keep.

Reliability

Spacing, clear space, color values, and minimum size are documented. The same lockup works in the app, on a home screen, and in print.

Motion

A brief, gentle turn or glimmer may add delight on interaction. Motion is never required to understand the mark and is removed for reduced-motion preferences.

Honest craft

Gradients enhance the finish but do not carry the identity. The mark remains recognizable as flat color, monochrome, or a simple outline.

The finished identity should feel like a treasured book opened at home: considered enough to be regal, kind enough to be shared, and joyful enough to return to.
